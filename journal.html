ENTRY 00125 September 2026Shadowglen, Teldrassil

Leaving Shadowglen

I arrived with nothing worth calling a fortune. Now I am leaving Aldrassil and Shadowglen behind at level six with exactly six silver and sixty-eight copper to my name. Somehow, that feels right.

I've learned every spell Ayanna can teach me for now. I traded some of what little coin I'd earned for a Dink Dagger, a Hornwood Recurve Bow and a full set of cracked leather — chest, legs, wrists, hands, waist and feet. I bought a small eight-slot quiver, an Apprentice Skinning Satchel, a skinning knife, milk and versicolour treats when food proved harder to find than I expected, and enough rough arrows that I now carry fifteen hundred of the bloody things. Six gold stands between me and the road beyond Teldrassil, but I won't reach it by starving myself of everything I need along the way.

I've taken up Herbalism and Skinning. Before leaving Shadowglen I reached the limit of what I can learn as an Apprentice Skinner — 75 skill — while my Herbalism has reached 20. The forests provide plenty if I pay attention. I'll keep Peacebloom, Bruiseweed, Briarthorn, Liferoot, Sungrass and Golden Sansam when I find them; one day an alchemist may turn them into medicines that keep me alive. Other herbs can be traded away when coin would serve me better. I leave with twenty-two Peacebloom tucked safely into my pack, along with some linen cloth and a cloak I managed to scavenge.

It is strange how quickly everything begins to have a purpose. A flower tucked into my pack might become medicine months from now. A hide taken from a beast might buy my next meal, my next arrow, or a warmer piece of armour. Six gold sounds like a fortune tonight, but perhaps that is the point. I do not want simply to pass through this place. I want to leave it ready for whatever waits beyond.

Every task I could find in Shadowglen is finished. Somewhere among my travels I also found a journal that speaks of learning how to make a proper camp. I have kept it. If I am really going to live on the road, perhaps it is time I learned.

Tonight I leave Aldrassil and Shadowglen behind. My purse is light, my pack smells faintly of herbs and hides, and the road to Dolanaar waits beyond the trees. It feels like a good place to close the first page.

A THOUGHT BEFORE I GO...

I keep hearing other adventurers boast about finding coloured linen bags tucked among beasts and bushes. Apparently everyone in Shadowglen is tripping over spare luggage except me. Could I find one? Could I heck. My poor pack is already being asked to hold hides, herbs, cloth, food and enough arrows to arm half of Teldrassil. I really need to do something about storage soon.

IN THE MARGIN

Six gold. Right now I have six silver and sixty-eight copper. I wonder whether I’ll laugh at that number when I finally leave Teldrassil… or curse whoever decided it was enough.

ENTRY 00225 September 2026Dolanaar & Starbreeze Village, Teldrassil

The Road to Dolanaar

I think I am beginning to understand what life beyond Shadowglen is actually going to require of me.

I arrived in Dolanaar with little coin and far too much stuffed into far too few pockets. Apparently complaining about my lack of bags worked — just not quite in the way I expected.

One of the locals offered me a proper six-slot Soft Saber Pouch if I could bring back six soft pelts. Considering the little brown pouches being sold nearby cost around five silver — practically everything I had left after learning First Aid — I was hardly going to turn that down.

Six pelts later, I finally have a bag.

A whole six slots. Luxury.

So no, I still haven't found one of those mysterious coloured linen bags everyone else apparently pulls from bushes.

But mine is softer. And blue!

I'm choosing to consider that a victory.

Dolanaar has taught me something more important than where to put all my things, though. I've finally learned how to make a proper camp. Until now, the road has mostly been somewhere between one place and another. I think that's going to change. A fire means somewhere to rest, somewhere to prepare, somewhere to eat — and perhaps somewhere another traveller might stop for a while too.

I've also begun learning First Aid, and those Peaceblooms I've been carefully stuffing into my pack finally have a purpose. With a few empty vials, I can turn them into health potions. Twenty-two flowers suddenly feel considerably more valuable than they did yesterday.

Food has been another problem. I bought milk and treats before leaving Shadowglen because finding enough to eat was becoming annoyingly unreliable, but buying every meal isn't going to get me very far when I'm trying to scrape together six gold. I've been given the chance to improve my Cooking, and I found a recipe for Brilliant Smallfish for only forty copper. I could learn it immediately, so I bought it. If the rivers and lakes are willing to feed me, I'd be an idiot to keep handing merchants my silver for snacks.

Maybe soon my campfire will actually smell like dinner instead of smoke and desperation.

I found another herb today — Mageroyal. Unfortunately, finding it and being capable of harvesting it are apparently two entirely different things. My hands aren't skilled enough yet, so for now all I can do is remember where I've seen it and try not to look too offended when I walk past.

I did overhear an Alchemist talking about mixing Mageroyal with Peacebloom to make mana potions. Now that caught my attention. My abilities draw on mana, and running dry in the middle of a fight is hardly ideal. Perhaps Mageroyal is worth keeping when I'm finally able to gather it. If I can supply an Alchemist with the herbs they need, there may be a trade to be made — herbs for potions, rather than everything simply becoming another few copper in a merchant's pocket.

NOTE TO SELF

Learn to pick the bloody purple flowers.

I've started finding Light Leather now too. The ruined scraps I've been collecting are another matter. I still don't know how to salvage anything useful from them, and carrying rubbish around because it might become useful someday seems like an excellent way to recreate my bag problem, so I've been selling them.

If I learn differently later, so be it. For now, coin is useful and ruined leather isn't.

My journey took me through Starbreeze Village, where I found myself helping with the troubles there. While we were dealing with that, something much larger decided to make itself our problem: Uruson, a furbolg driven mad by fel moss.

I wasn't alone when we fought him. Several of us turned on the creature and eventually brought him down. By the time the fighting stopped there was nothing on him for me to claim.

I don't particularly care.

I didn't draw my bow for his pockets.

Before setting out again, I made one last stop for supplies. I bought some mild spices for cooking and a handful of empty vials, then used some of the Peacebloom I'd carried all the way from Shadowglen to brew health potions for the journey.

My work in Dolanaar isn't finished. There are still people here who need help, paths through Teldrassil I haven't walked and far too much of this forest I haven't seen. But I've been asked to travel to Darnassus.

I think I'll go.

Perhaps a stroll through the great city is exactly what I need before returning to the road.

I arrived here with barely enough silver to buy one of those little brown pouches hanging from the merchants' stalls. I'm leaving for Darnassus with eighteen silver and seventy-eight copper in my purse.

I can build myself a camp. I can cook my own meals. I can gather what grows around me, dress my wounds and brew medicine for the road. I have food, arrows, health potions and a little coin tucked away for whatever comes next.

And, most importantly, I finally have a proper bag.

It's softer. And blue.

Priorities.

IN THE MARGIN

Six gold still sounds ridiculous.

But yesterday I had six silver.

Today I have eighteen.

Perhaps the road pays better than I thought.

ENTRY 00325 September 2026Darnassus, Teldrassil

Beneath the Boughs of Darnassus

On the road to Darnassus, I came across a house belonging to a Skinner and Leatherworker. That may prove dangerous.

The Leatherworker offered to teach me their trade, and for the first time since taking up Herbalism I found myself seriously considering putting down the gardening shears.

It makes sense. I already spend much of my time skinning the beasts I hunt, and I've started putting Light Leather aside without really knowing what I'll eventually do with it. Being able to turn those hides into something useful myself feels like the sort of skill that could serve me well on the road.

I'm not quite ready to abandon Herbalism yet, though. Before I make that decision, I've begun storing Peacebloom away. Forty flowers are sitting safely in my bank now, along with the Light Leather I've collected. If I do trade the gardening shears for a Leatherworker's tools, I'd rather have a good supply tucked away first.

Besides, putting everything in the bank achieved something almost as precious as the materials themselves.

Bag space.

Beautiful, glorious, empty bag space.

While I was there, I overheard a Mage and a Rogue discussing bags. Apparently those coloured linen bags I've heard everyone talking about are often found among the nightsabers.

Interesting.

Very interesting.

Perhaps the bushes were innocent all along.

The Skinning trainer had more to teach me than simply taking hides. He showed me how to make a camp chair, which gives me a better chance of landing a particularly good strike while resting at camp.

Two percent isn't enormous.

But two percent is considerably more than zero.

He also agreed to teach me the ways of a Journeyman Skinner.

For five silver.

I am beginning to suspect that civilisation is simply the practice of charging people money for knowing things.

Still, I've outgrown what I could learn as an Apprentice. If better hides lie ahead, I need to know how to work with them.

Then I finally reached Darnassus.

I don't think Shadowglen prepared me for it.

The city feels as though it grew from Teldrassil rather than being built upon it. Great stone columns rise between enormous boughs, purple light settles across the paths, and everywhere I look there is another archway, pool or ancient structure disappearing into the trees.

For somewhere so enormous, it doesn't feel as though the forest ever truly ends.

It is beautiful.

Majestic, even.

It is also bloody expensive.

Five silver disappeared on a desperately needed new bag. Another six went towards three new hunting techniques. I've learned to parry more effectively, put considerably more force behind my Raptor Strike, and even imbue an arrow with magic to deliver a Concussive Shot.

That last one is interesting. It slows my prey, which could be extremely useful against quicker creatures — nightsabers and spiders immediately come to mind — but it takes a sizeable bite from my mana.

Probably not something I should fire at everything that looks at me funny.

Useful, though. Very useful.

Especially if those rumours about nightsabers carrying bags turn out to be true.

The walk back to Dolanaar gave me plenty of time to think.

Mostly about Leatherworking.

Partly about how much silver Darnassus stole from me.

By the time I reached Dolanaar, I was tired enough that both could wait until morning.

I stopped by a merchant first and emptied my packs of anything I couldn't see myself needing. A few useful-looking things became a few more coins, my bags became a little lighter, and by the time I finally counted my purse I had eight silver and forty-six copper.

Not exactly a fortune.

But I left Shadowglen with six silver and sixty-eight copper, and since then I've bought supplies, learned new skills, paid for training, filled my bank with herbs and leather, improved my bags and somehow become a Journeyman Skinner.

And I still have more coin than I started with.

I suppose that's a plus.

For tonight, though, I've had quite enough roads, forests, cities, trainers and merchants.

There's a bed waiting for me at the inn.

The rest of Teldrassil can wait until morning.

IN THE MARGIN

Level nine.

Eight silver and forty-six copper.

Twenty-four empty bag spaces.

Forty Peacebloom safely tucked away.

Still richer than when I left Shadowglen.

I'll call that progress.

Now I'm going for a kip.

ENTRY 00425 September 2026Dolanaar & the forests of Teldrassil

The Ways of the Beast

The day began quietly enough. I spent the morning camping in the woods with other travellers, sitting around the fire and talking about the world and the journeys that had brought us here.

Then I went to Starbreeze Village and killed an unreasonable number of mad furbolgs.

Level ten.

Apparently that was enough for word to reach Dazalar, a Master Hunter who believed I was finally ready to learn the ways of the beasts.

His first lesson?

Tame a spider.

A fucking spider.

I had just finished dealing with satyrs who kept enormous red spiders as guards, and now I was expected to convince one that we were friends.

For something with eight legs, the bloody thing was surprisingly slow. I thought spiders were supposed to skitter. Still, I decided that merely taming him wasn't enough and took him with me into Ban'ethil Hollow.

He wasn't particularly sturdy, but his poison proved useful. We started finding a rhythm together, and when he eventually succumbed to his injuries, I felt his absence while fighting my way back out.

I'd spent the entire time complaining about him. Apparently I'd grown attached anyway.

Ban'ethil wasn't entirely miserable. I crossed paths with several other travellers, eventually joining a group of three Priests, three Hunters and two Rogues.

Calling us organised would be generous.

We stormed through anything in our way with what I can only describe as Night Elf military tactics:

There it is. Get it.

Unorganised chaos.

I'm here for it.

Somewhere among all of this, I was asked what kind of Hunter I wanted to become.

After the spider, the answer seemed obvious.

Beast Mastery.

Dazalar's next test was considerably more agreeable: a nightsaber.

Not a kitty.

A murder kitty.

Much more my kind of beast.

Then came a Strigid Owl. Birds aren't really my thing, but I'll admit the little bastard was cute.

Spider. Nightsaber. Owl.

Apparently that was enough.

Dazalar has finally taught me how to tame a beast of my own.

I'm going to wait.

I still don't properly understand what different beasts need to eat, and I won't learn that until I'm a little more experienced. I'm not asking a creature to trust me enough to leave its home when I can't even guarantee I know how to feed it.

My companion can wait until I'm ready.

Plenty else changed today too. I can finally harvest Mageroyal — the bloody purple flowers have been conquered — and I've upgraded from Rough to Sharp Arrows. Those 1,600 Rough Arrows I bought carried me all the way to level ten, so naturally I've celebrated this sensible financial decision by buying a new quiver capable of holding 2,000.

I've also improved my Serpent Sting and learned a stronger Aspect of the Hawk, borrowing something of the hawk's instinct to strengthen my ranged attacks.

And somewhere along the road, I met Maise again.

At this point we've stopped pretending normal greetings are necessary.

"MAISE!!!!"

"CANDEI!!!!"

Teldrassil is enormous. I have no idea why we keep finding each other.

I hope it continues.

I've treated myself to a full set of Rough Leather armour, another six-slot bag and even earned myself a new dagger after dealing with those disgusting satyrs and their enormous red spiders.

Now I'm supposed to be heading back towards Darnassus for more Hunter training.

Supposed to be.

Because on my way I came across a group of less experienced adventurers preparing to make their way towards Ban'ethil Burrow Den.

I've already seen what waits down there. I've fought through those tunnels with a beast at my side, fought my way out without him, and watched a small army of Night Elves turn the place into complete chaos.

I couldn't very well watch them walk into it alone.

Darnassus isn't going anywhere.

They might need another bow.

So I'll help them through first.

Then — assuming we all come back out again — I'll continue to the city.

IN THE MARGIN

I was going to Darnassus.

Then I saw some newer adventurers heading towards Ban'ethil.

I know what's waiting for them.

Bugger.

Darnassus can wait.

— Candei ✦
